A day in the life

Computing that knows when to disappear.

Mynah is a family of quiet physical and conversational interfaces woven into ordinary life: waking, learning, working, caring for a home, raising children, and living together.

Most of the day, most of the devices should be quiet.

5:45 AM

The day begins before anyone touches a screen.

The Mynah Ring has been sensing sleep, movement, heart rate, HRV and temperature through the night. Nothing demands attention. The data simply becomes background context for the morning.

SleepHRVTemperatureMovement

Personal cycle information can be part of that context too when someone chooses to track it. Like the rest of Mynah's intimate data, it belongs to that person unless they deliberately share it.

6:25 AM

Astrolabe notices that you are near.

You approach Astrolabe. RSSI from the Ring rises, and the ambient astronomical face quietly becomes your LunaSay face. You do not need to touch it.

Far away → ambient. Nearby → personal. Pause → detail.

LunaSay gives orientation rather than notifications: sleep, time, calendar, astronomy, and whatever personal rhythms you have chosen to track.

It can also offer a light family-synastry reading. Perhaps the household looks better suited to independent mornings and a shared evening. It does not tell anyone how they will feel. It simply offers another symbolic way to notice timing and relationship.

6:40 AM

You carry Alpheus with you.

You pick up Mynah Person and press the side button.

“Alpheus, morning.”

Person is the private, mobile voice into Alpheus, Castalia's sovereign AI. It is small on purpose: good for capture, commands, short conversations and control; bad at becoming somewhere to disappear for an hour.

6:45 AM

The kitchen wakes around you.

Mynah Room raises the lights and starts quiet music. The Atlas station wakes. Then the printer starts moving.

No dashboard appears. The room simply becomes ready for morning.

6:50 AM

The morning edition arrives on paper.

The Gazette says what matters today: weather, schedule, sunrise and sunset, a few relevant stories, and things the household asked to remember.

The Gazetteer says what is happening here: what is flowering, migrating, flowing, changing, or worth noticing nearby.

Today's question: Where could a frog find water near us?

The paper ends. There is no feed underneath it.

7:15 AM

The children build a question with their hands.

At breakfast, the children choose from a bowl of small bamboo iNQ Coins. Each one is 35 mm across, only 1.3 mm thick, and marked with a black sumi-e glyph.

35 mm · 1.3 mm · bamboo · sumi-e glyph · NFC

One child chooses FROG. Another chooses WATER. They place both on Atlas.

Frog + Water → Where could a frog find water here today?

The coins are not flash cards. They are a tangible language for combining ideas before a child needs to read or type.

7:30 AM

Sometimes Alpheus answers with another question.

A child calls across the room: “Alpheus, do frogs sleep?” Alpheus answers briefly, then hands the curiosity back to Atlas.

Where do you think a frog would choose to rest?

The point is not to keep the conversation with the AI. The point is to send the child back toward the world.

8:00 AM

Atlas goes outside.

Atlas is built around a Samsung Galaxy A16 in a protective field case with a specimen-tray lid. The tray carries the day's iNQ Coins and gives the children a place for appropriate field finds—stones, fallen leaves, shells, seed pods—while the cameras document what they encounter.

The children carry Atlas, the Gazetteer, FROG and WATER outside. Someone adds INSECT.

Water + Insect → Would insects make this a better place for frogs?
8:20 AM

Atlas teaches how to know.

A child points Atlas toward a plant. Atlas may suggest an identification, but it also points out the evidence: leaf shape, stem, fruit, flower, habitat.

The lesson is not that the computer knows the name. The lesson is how a naturalist could know.

A beetle disappears under a log. A child says, “It went underneath.” Atlas saves the observation. It can remain Unknown beetle #3 for as long as necessary.

8:30 AM

The parent is available without hovering.

An adult's Ring and Person establish that help is nearby. When the question becomes safety-sensitive, Atlas can ask for a parent.

Atlas requests adult help: berry identification.

Otherwise, the inquiry remains the child's.

9:00 AM

A thought disappears into Person instead of a phone.

“Alpheus, remember this: compare this year's frog observations with last spring.”

Done. No app, no keyboard, no inbox. Person is for capturing intent while it is still small.

10:00 AM

Later, eInq is where thought slows down.

At a desk, Mynah eInq receives the fragments that actually deserve attention: the frog note, the children's observations, perhaps something from the Gazette.

Here you read, annotate, compare sources and develop ideas.

Person captures. eInq considers. Atlas investigates.
12:00 PM

Lunch is allowed to be lunch.

Room quiets. Atlas docks. eInq closes. The printed Gazette remains on the table. Nobody needs to put the household into “family mode.” The context has already changed.

How much useful computing happened today without requiring anyone to stare at a screen?
1:15 PM

The same Astrolabe becomes someone else's.

A spouse approaches and their Ring signal rises. Astrolabe becomes their LunaSay face without exposing anyone else's private notes or physiology.

If they track menstrual-cycle information, it can appear here alongside sleep, temperature and calendar context. It remains theirs. If useful, they may later choose to bring part of that context into a conversation or MARIA session.

1:30 PM

The children change the question.

Back at Atlas, FROG returns. Then NIGHT. Then SOUND.

Frog + Night + Sound → What might we hear after dark?

Atlas suggests an evening listening expedition. Three small wooden coins have turned into a new investigation.

3:30 PM

Atlas keeps observation and reality separate.

At a zoo, a child places the ZEBRA coin on Atlas. Atlas can record where the animal was observed while keeping that location distinct from its natural range.

A map is not the territory. A photograph is not a specimen. Recognition is not certainty. Those distinctions are part of the education.

5:15 PM

The day comes back together without becoming a feed.

Person has accumulated a few voice fragments. Atlas has observations. eInq offers only what might deserve a second look. Most of it can disappear.

Capture should be easy. Accumulation should be optional.

6:30 PM

Dinner is mostly non-computational.

Room handles lighting and music. Alpheus is available through Room or Person but says nothing unless invited. Atlas is docked. eInq is closed.

The family has been using Mynah all day, yet most of dinner contains no visible computing at all.

7:30 PM

The children choose what the day meant.

Atlas briefly returns. On the table are a few of the day's iNQ Coins: FROG, WATER, INSECT, ZEBRA.

Which one do you want to remember?

A child chooses INSECT. Atlas plays back: “It went underneath the log.” Add HOME, and the memory becomes another question:

Insect + Home → What makes underneath the log a good home?

No points. No streak. No badge.

8:15 PM

Theritor turns writing back toward life.

Later, two adults sit down with MARIA, the marital-therapy persona within Theritor. Theritor knows the author's manuscripts and recurring ideas, as well as whatever relationship material both partners have deliberately shared.

MARIA might notice that today's conflict resembles a principle already explored in the books and ask whether that principle applies at home.

The books become mirrors, not weapons.

With permission, physiological context or LunaSay's synastry can enter as additional lenses. They remain lenses—not explanations.

9:30 PM

An adult question becomes tomorrow's child question.

Something from the children's morning still bothers you in a good way. You read for a while on eInq, then tell Person:

“Alpheus, put this on Atlas tomorrow: why does that plant grow here?”

Adult curiosity becomes material for the children's next investigation.

10:30 PM

The last interface is barely an interface.

You approach Astrolabe. Ring RSSI rises. LunaSay shows a few things worth remembering and perhaps a gentle note about tomorrow.

You walk away. The personal face disappears.

Room settles the house. Person charges. Atlas sleeps. The Ring stays on.

The roles

Each object protects a different kind of attention.

The day works because no single device tries to become the whole system.

Presence

Mynah Ring

Identity, nearness, sleep, movement and physiological context.

Orientation

Astrolabe / LunaSay

Time, astronomy, personal rhythms, family synastry and reflection.

Intent

Mynah Person

Private mobile access to Alpheus, capture, communication and control.

Place

Mynah Room

Shared voice, lighting, sound, local devices and the state of a room.

Voice

Alpheus

Castalia's sovereign conversational intelligence, available through Person and Room without replacing specialized experiences.

Inquiry

Atlas + iNQ Coins

A Samsung A16 field instrument and tangible sumi-e vocabulary for child-led inquiry.

Thought

Mynah eInq

Reading, annotation, synthesis and sustained attention.

Relationship

Theritor / MARIA

Editorial intelligence turned toward reflection on a shared life.

The platform underneath

Mynah Context

The interfaces are different apertures into one contextual model: person, relationship, place, time, physiology, object, inquiry, observation, source, memory, permission and action.

One governing rule keeps the system coherent: measurement, interpretation, symbolism and private reflection are different kinds of information. Mynah can connect them, but it does not pretend they are the same thing.

Presence does not require interaction.
Context can move freely across devices. It must cross human boundaries deliberately.
